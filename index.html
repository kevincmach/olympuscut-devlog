The game

what the judges will hold in their hands

You are Zeus, top-down on an island arena. Survive the sheep swarm, face the Great Ram, then hunt the Cyclops — three acts, each under different light, each with its own twist. Before every act you make one story edit; as you level you write epithets — Homeric titles — into the text. Combat runs on a rhythm: land hits on the gold beat ring for bonus damage, thread a thunderbolt through the Cyclops' eye while he kneels. Win or die, the run ends on Your Director's Cut: Homer's version beside yours.

THE THEME, TAKEN LITERALLY

Most games would name a difficulty "Director's Cut." This one makes you the editor of a canonical text. The Odyssey's Cyclops episode is public domain — so the game treats it as source footage. Your three story picks per run are edits: give him wine, steal his flock, say your name is Nobody. Each one rewrites how the next act plays, and the trick Odysseus actually used — "Nobody" — is in the card deck.

Every run ends the same way regardless of outcome: the ending sets Homer's lines against your version, act by act, closing with "… and the Cyclops fell" or "… and was struck from the story." Death is just a cut Zeus doesn't survive.

The journey

the jam, told the way the game plays it — three acts, an overnight run, and an epilogue · deep dives open on click

ACT I — THE GREYBOXDay 1 · Thu Nov 12 · daylight

The repo held nothing but a gitignore and a README at noon. By 12:52 the arena kit stood — sand, water ring, cave, ruins — with Zeus and a four-metre Cyclops facing each other. Twelve minutes later the full boss fight ran: chain lightning into the Cyclops, both health bars live, a 10-second clip captured as proof.

The afternoon compounded: audio wired, camera pulled back after a “too close” note, telegraph discs lifted out of the ground, XP and level-up cards, then touch controls with a stick and dodge button. A headless TypeScript balance sim (built in Cursor) replaced guesswork with numbers, and by 16:40 the first iPhone build ran at a locked 60 fps.

In the evening the Director's Cut feel pass landed: a 3-hit hold-to-attack combo with thunderclap, two-charge dash, Wrath meter, hitstop, knockback, flash and FOV punch. On the phone by 21:55.

The models that rendered as invisible specksStep 4 · day 1

Zeus and the Cyclops arrived as FBX exports with their own skeletons. First play-test: no models at all. The first diagnosis was wrong — the meshes were set HiddenInGame from the placeholder era; fixing that still showed nothing.

The real cause: the FBX files were authored in metres and the importer did not convert to centimetres. Zeus's bounds came in at ~1.2 units tall — a speck. Both characters now run at component scale 100. Lesson written into the log: call get_bounds right after import; a zero-error log does not mean a visible asset.

The same step caught a subtler killer: spawned sheep had no AI controller (Auto Possess was set to Placed-in-World only), so non-dashing sheep never walked, the wave never ended, and the boss never spawned. Reproduced at 63 s with two sheep alive and bBossSpawned=false; fixed with “Placed in World or Spawned.”

Telegraphs buried under the floorStep 12 · 13:49

Kevin kept taking damage “for nothing.” The evidence: every telegraph disc — the red warning circles that are the game's fairness contract — spawned at z=48 under a floor at z=75. All warnings were underground and invisible. Discs were raised +42; sheep contact range tightened 130 → 100 cm; a red full-screen hit flash added so no hit is ever silent again.

Balanced by simulation, not vibesSteps 8 & 16 · 15:28

Kevin's day-one verdict: “the game is way too easy.” The first fix (boss 1,000 → 2,600 HP) was still vibes. So a headless TypeScript simulator was built in Cursor — 60 Hz, 2,000 runs per player policy — and the game's every number went through it.

The feel pass: “it now feels like a game”Step 19 · 21:46

“Feel” was enumerated, not vibes: a 3-hit combo with 0.30/0.35/0.45 s gaps, hitstop of 0.04 s (0.12 on big hits, max one per 0.1 s), knockback 260 cm/s on bolts and 1100 + 420 up on heavies, gold damage numbers at 1.7× size on big hits, FOV punches of 5–7°, procedural Zeus lean (10° pitch, 8° roll) and Cyclops squash-and-bob.

The pass also fixed its own bug before commit: FOV punch and the Wrath camera pull never decayed, because FeelUpdate read its “last time” variable before the interps — pure nodes re-evaluate per use, so real-time delta was always 0. Order swapped, decay restored.

Installed on the iPhone at 21:55. Kevin's verdict: “a huge difference.”

ACT II — THE HUNTDay 2 · Fri Nov 13 · night

Just after midnight the story shell appeared: a title screen, a pick of three story edits before each act, three acts on one map under day, night and storm light, and the Director's Cut ending assembled from the picks — death included, with its own line.

Then the hunt itself: a stagger bar that topples the Cyclops for four seconds of double damage, a Leap Slam whose shadow chases Zeus, and combat deepened in three steps — a Brawl Stars-style aim stick and the gold beat ring, body-part targeting (eye, club, legs), and the Thunderbolt with the once-per-topple eye strike.

The day's hardest bug wasn't combat: manual aiming gave Kevin a headache. The cause was the spring arm's built-in lag running on world time — every hitstop froze and released the camera. The camera now follows on real time from 1900 cm at −60°, and held perfectly still while Zeus fired.

The boss that forgot half his attacksSteps 21 & 25 · day 2

Symptom, found in code before Kevin ever felt it: the Cyclops only ever cycled Shockwave and Boulder. Bellow never fired.

Cause: the attack tick set AttackIndex = i+1 and then branched on a pure Get of the same variable — so the check read the new value. From index 1 it set 2, saw 2+1 ≥ 3, reset to 0, and fired Shockwave. The cycle was Shockwave ↔ Boulder forever. PIE evidence agreed: the stored index only ever read 0 or 1, and hit logs alternated 240/200 with no summoned sheep.

Fixed in Step 25 by calling DoAttack(AttackIndex) before incrementing. The same pure-node trap was found in the telegraph wind-up (resolving one frame early, ~16 ms — ignored) — a whole class of Blueprint bug written into the log so it wouldn't be re-made.

Every hit fair and visibleSteps 20–21 · 22:50

Kevin: “even when I'm outside the red circles I still get damaged.” The fix started with instrumentation — every hit logged with source and amount. One run, standing still: 58 of 66 hits were invisible sheep body contact, then 3 boulders, 3 rings, 2 dash discs. “I did nothing” was actually three sheep overlapping him at 120 per 0.55 s each.

Fixes: 0.6 s hit grace (measured after: 340 applied hits, minimum gap 0.667 s), a white flash plus a red hurt number on every hit, discs vanishing 0.15 s after landing, and the ring's hitbox pulled inside its visible edge.

The camera that ran on world timeStep 28b · 15:31

After the aim-stick step, Kevin got a headache: the screen “juddered” while fighting. Systematic ruling-out: turning to aim doesn't rotate the boom; lean only moves the mesh; Zeus's own attacks already didn't shake.

Found: the spring arm's built-in lag ran on world time with speed 5 / max 150 cm — and every hit triggers a hitstop, world dilation 0.02 for 0.04 s. On-beat play lands 2–3 hits a second, so while Zeus moved, the camera's lag froze and caught up in a stop-and-slide rhythm. Wrath's 320 cm zoom-out on every cast amplified it.

Fix: arm lag off; the follow runs on real-time delta clamped to 0.1 s, from 1900 cm at −60° (was 1500/−52°), showing ±1,190 cm — most of BOLT's 1,400 range. Proof, measured in PIE: 14 position samples over 2.8 s of on-beat fire: 0.0 cm of camera movement.

Aim sticks, the beat, and hold-to-fireSteps 27–28d

Kevin found hold-to-attack boring: “it played the game for him.” The rework made every shot a decision — ATTACK became an aim stick, and the beat arrived: after each shot a window opens at 0.22 s and closes at 0.57 s. On-beat = +25% and a chain toward the thunderclap; mashing before 0.22 s = fumble. Measured with the on-beat autopilot: 61 on-beat hits and 20 thunderclaps in 35 s; the mash tester earned 8 fumbles and zero bonuses.

Twin-stick aim (28c): Zeus was swinging 720°/s and only faced shots when nearly stationary — he now snaps to the stick every frame and strafes. Measured: stick down turns him 90° → 180° on the first frame.

Hold-to-fire returned by Kevin's own choice (28d): holding fires a plain shot every ~0.67 s, always just after the beat window — hold = 8 shots in 5.1 s, never a fumble; re-tapping on the gold ring chains on-beat hits. Depth for both play styles.

Body parts: where you stand is what you breakStep 28 · 15:17

Each hit lands on a part by Zeus's position relative to the Cyclops's facing: head (±25°, ×1.2), back (×1.3), legs inside 400 cm, club on his weapon side. He turns at only 70°/s — measured 69.4 — so circling him is the game.

One millimetre of textStep 29c · 00:30

Kevin on the iPhone: “i can't read any of the text on the level up card.” The level-up screen was the last HUD piece in fixed pixels — on a dense ~1,200 px canvas the card text rendered about 1 mm tall.

The rebuild: cards scale with the screen, one centred in each tap third, big number / title / description shrunk to fit. A colour trap surfaced and was logged: Canvas MakeColor is linear but converts to sRGB on output, so a “dark” 0.40 reads as tan — the marble-skin inks use linear 0.10 / 0.02.

The maps that never cookedStep 30 · 11:40

On the iPhone, Kevin couldn't finish Act 1: “the game restarts me over… i never even see the boss battle.”

Cause: the cook contained only the default map. With no maps-to-cook list, Act 1's OpenLevel "Island" found nothing in the package and fell back to the default map — Act 1 restarted forever. PIE loads every map from disk, which is exactly why it only broke on the phone. Fix: +MapsToCook for Pasture, Island and Cave; the cooked AssetRegistry then listed all three.

Follow-up the same day: menu labels went blank on device because two imported FontFace assets had never been saved — the editor showed them, the disk did not. Logged lesson: after importing, check the files on disk, not the editor.

The killing blow, timed to the frameStep 33 · 15:30

The death moment is a stack of explicit timings, all on real-time clocks so the ×0.2 slow-mo can't stretch them: gold flash up to 60% and gone by 0.35 s; a 10.5° FOV punch easing out over 0.6 s (the comfort pass had zeroed punches, so the kill path carries its own); the eye light and emissive fading over 1.2 s while he holds his pose and sinks 30 cm — no ragdoll; banner at +0.3 s held 2.0 s; thunder, the score ducked to 30%, haptic 8 (the strongest single pulse). An eye-strike kill reads “BLINDED!” over “THE GIANT FALLS”.

Measured during the kill: frame 14.3 ms (game 3.8, GPU 13.8) — far inside the 33 ms a 30 fps cap allows.

EPILOGUE — SHIP TO HARDWARESat Nov 14 · 12:15–17:15 · on the Z Fold 7

The Android pipeline was assembled pin by pin: the engine's exact NDK (r27c — the installed r27b sits below the pinned minimum), official command-line tools, and one genuine villain — Gradle 8.13 refusing to run on Android Studio's brand-new Java 25 runtime, dying with “Unsupported class file major version 69” 29 seconds into packaging. Temurin 17 ended it; the 207 MB APK installed on the Fold at 12:36.

Then the phone taught the rest: touch was dead because every phone path was gated on iOS — seven checks opened to Android; the near-square foldable inner screen needed a resizable window and a clamped HUD scale; and the act banner returned on temple arrival because elapsed time went negative when each level's clock restarted at zero. Found on the Fold, fixed within the hour, re-tested on the Fold.

Seven gates, one platform checkStep 31 · 12:30

First Fold launch: “none of the touch or controls work.” The game ran its Mac mouse-and-keys path on Android because every phone gate tested GetPlatformName == "IOS" — the phone-controls doc even said so out loud: “Touch input is only read on iOS.”

Seven checks across BP_Zeus.PhoneUpdate, MacAim, three haptic paths and DrawTouch became Contains("IOS,Android", platform). One submission-level decision logged with it: the APK stays a Development build, because the gameplay telegraphs draw with DrawDebug, and Shipping strips all of it.

The safe zone that shrank the HUDStep 32 · 13:57

On the Fold's cover screen the red hurt border “is not flush with the edges… kinda shrunk in.” Cause: Android reports the camera hole as a 4% safe zone; UE shifts and shrinks the whole HUD canvas to clear it — it moves the canvas but doesn't crop it. The inner screen has no cutout, which is why it looked right there.

Fix: DrawHurtEdge measures the shift each frame (viewport size − canvas size) and draws each strip at the real screen edge. With no safe zone it draws exactly as before; the other HUD elements keep the shift on purpose, so they stay clear of the camera.

Grey text, silenced with an A/B testStep 36 · 16:50

PrintStrings and engine warnings were printing grey debug text over the phone build. The GameInstance now runs DisableAllScreenMessages at startup — it outlives every level load, and the exec path works even with no world yet (GEngine->Exec(nullptr) handles it).

Proven with an A/B capture: with messages disabled, a forced PERFECT DODGE drew nothing; re-enabled, the same call drew the text plus a grey engine warning. Frame time held at 14.9 ms (game 4.2–4.5, GPU 13.9–14.3) under the 33 ms 30 fps budget.

Forensics

four bugs worth stealing — each one found with evidence, killed with a measurement

THE INVISIBLE HITS“I feel like I'm still getting hit… with nothing on the screen.”

Evidence: every hit logged with source and amount. One idle run: 58 of 66 hits were invisible sheep contact; every warning disc spawned 27 cm under the floor.

Fix: discs raised above ground; 0.6 s hit grace; white flash + red number on every hit; discs vanish 0.15 s after landing.

Proof: 340 subsequent hits, minimum gap 0.667 s — the grace holds.

BELLOW NEVER FIRESThe boss cycled two of his four attacks — invisibly.

Cause: a Blueprint pure-node trap — the attack index was incremented and then re-read by the branch, so the check always saw the next value and wrapped early.

Fix: call the attack before the increment. The whole bug class (pure nodes re-evaluate per use) was written into the log after it also surfaced in the telegraph timer and the FOV decay.

Proof: the stored index had only ever read 0 or 1; after the fix, all four attacks cycle in PIE.

THE CAMERA ON WORLD TIME“Manual aiming gave me a headache.”

Cause: spring-arm lag integrated on world time; every hitstop (dilation 0.02, 2–3 per second on-beat) froze and released the follow — a stop-and-slide judder, plus a 320 cm Wrath zoom on every cast.

Fix: arm lag off; the follow interpolates on real-time delta from 1900 cm at −60°.

Proof: 14 samples over 2.8 s of on-beat fire — 0.0 cm of camera movement.

THE BUTTONS THAT MOVED LEFT“Attack works but dash, bolt and wrath don't trigger.” — Fold 7, cover screen

Cause: Android reports the camera hole as a ~4% safe zone; the HUD canvas is shifted to clear it, but touch coordinates arrive in raw viewport pixels. Every hit circle sat ~100 px left of its drawn button. ATTACK's 148 px radius still caught a centred press — the 94 px buttons never did. The inner screen has no cutout, so it only broke folded.

Fix: SafeShift measures the canvas shift every frame and subtracts it from every touch — 80 consumers re-routed in one pass.

Proof, on-device: an injected touch 100 px left of the BOLT art logged “[Bolt] thrown”; after the fix, taps on the drawn art land.

THE ONE THAT ALMOST GOT AWAY

First Android package died in 29 seconds: Unsupported class file major version 69. Class file 69 is Java 25 — the runtime that current Android Studio bundles, and one that Unreal's Gradle 8.13 cannot parse scripts under. Epic's own setup script had pointed JAVA_HOME at it automatically.

Fix: Temurin 17 pinned in the shell profile, engine-pinned versions passed to Epic's setup script (ndk;27.2.12479018, build-tools 36.0.0), and the one-line comment in .zshrc so no future build re-breaks it. Every claim here maps to a commit.
